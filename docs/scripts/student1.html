Student 1 - Opening & Problem Statement

Slides 1–3 | Time: 2 min 30 sec | Role: Set the context, hook the audience
"Before I start — how many of you have lost something on campus and had no idea where to look for it?"
Pause 2 seconds. Make eye contact with the audience. Let hands go up.
This opens with a question — grabs attention immediately.
"That is exactly the problem we are solving. Our project is called LAFSys — a Campus Lost and Found Board."
Point at the LAFSys title on the cover slide.
"It is a Java console application — posted, searched, and claimed — all from one place."
Gesture with open palm towards the tagline line on the cover.
"We are Group 5, from the Department of Computer Science, for the course Object-Oriented Programming with Java."
Point at GROUP 5 and course name on the cover slide.
"Let me show you why this matters."
Click to Slide 01 — The Problem. Step slightly to the side so the slide is visible.
"Every day on campus — items are lost. And most are never returned."
Point at the pull quote on the slide. Emphasize "most are never returned" with a slight hand gesture.
"This happens in hostels. Libraries. Labs. Cafeterias. Everywhere."
Count four locations on your fingers, one per word.
"There are five practical reasons this project exists."
Hold up five fingers. Then lower to point at the slide.
"Number one — no central place to record a lost item or report one that was found."
Point at problem 01 on the slide.
"Number two — notice boards are static and scattered across many buildings."
Point at problem 02.
"Number three — no way to search past reports. Users have to scroll and guess."
Point at problem 03.
"Number four — manual matching between finders and owners is slow and unreliable."
Point at problem 04.
"And number five — no record of resolution. So the same item gets asked about again and again."
Point at problem 05. Raise voice slightly on "again and again" for emphasis.
"So what did we build to fix this?"
Pause 1 second. Click to Slide 02 — Application Overview.
Transition question — keeps audience engaged.
"Four simple steps. Post. Save. Search. Claim."
Point at each of the four numbered steps on the slide, one by one.
"You post an item — enter the name, category, date, location, and contact."
Point at Step 1 — Post.
"It gets saved — appended to a file on disk. Not overwritten. Appended."
Point at Step 2 — Save. Emphasize "appended" with a hand motion showing adding to end.
"Anyone can search the list anytime. By keyword, by category."
Point at Step 3 — Search.
"And when the owner finds it — they claim it. Status changes from OPEN to RESOLVED."
Point at Step 4 — Claim.
"The five major features — Post, View, Search, Claim, and Persist."
Point at each of the five feature cards.
"That is the overview. Now over to my teammate to explain the Java concepts behind this."
Turn body towards next speaker. Make eye contact with them. Step back.
